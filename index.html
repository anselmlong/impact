Production incident: map previews

Raised and resolved 27 Oct

He noticed that map preview images on the maps.gov.sg homepage had stopped loading, and asked the team whether to declare an incident. It became SEV-3 incident 1479.

  • He traced the cause to an emergency Cloudflare WAF rule for a Next.js image-optimizer CVE, which the zone's override turned into a block. He then cut the pending release with the patched Next.js. It was resolved in about an hour E20
  • His manager's write-up in #eng credits him by name for spotting and debugging it, and puts the impact at about 63 users with no functional impact E21
  • Sourced from Slack only. Datadog could not be reached on 30 Oct or 6 Nov, so the incident record was not cross-checked.

Locate Me bug on the most-viewed map

Spotted 17 Oct

He found that the Locate Me button was broken in the climate-vouchers map embed, which brings in most of Maps' views, and reported it in #maps with a likely cause: the agency's iframe lacks geolocation permission.

  • The team declared it a SEV-4 on 19 Oct as incident 1458, "locate me fails on embedded maps" E18
  • Credit here is for spotting and diagnosing it. He is not recorded as the incident owner or as the person who fixed it.

The case

Why a return offer is justified

  1. He ships more than most of the team, adjusted for tenure. 31 merged PRs since his first on 19 Aug is 2.78 a week, against a team median of 0.60 (n = 7) E1. He slipped below the team's 75th percentile this week (2.99). Linear agrees from a separate source: 33 issues done, 2.90 a week against a team median of 2.16, with about the same median cycle time of 7 days E17. Ten of the merges came from one tech-debt week and nine are under 20 lines, so the count flatters him. His 13,920 lines merged are above the team median of 8,659. His PRs take longer to merge than the team median (29.7 hours against 26.1).
  2. He carries features from spec to guide. Stacked markers, ownership transfer and the editable map link each went out with a UAT doc he wrote, follow-up fixes, and an officer-guide PR E2 E3 E11. Across five UAT and bug-bash docs, including the search UAT, he logged 79 severity-tagged issues before release.
  3. He starts from evidence, and goes to users. Before building ingestion he measured the problem in production analytics, fixed his own first analysis when it hit the wrong project, ran moderated tests with officers from three agencies, and wrote an ADR with concrete safety limits E6 E7 E8. For the sidebar rework he measured scroll length on the 29 busiest public maps and tested it with three colleagues before asking for UAT E26.
  4. He measures what he ships. The Google Maps fix came with a 638-marker benchmark E5. He added search-outcome tracking (#911) and set up a Datadog monitor for blocked email recipients, then shipped the user-facing message for the same failure (#935) E15. He patched critical Next.js and DOMPurify advisories that were blocking PRs (#943). He also spotted the Locate Me failure on the most-viewed public map, which the team escalated to a SEV-4 E18. On 27 Oct he raised a SEV-3 on broken homepage map previews, found the cause and cut the fixing release within about an hour E20. His stacked-marker tracking (#922) merged on 26 Oct. In its first eleven days, production recorded 1,247 group-panel opens by 486 people outside the team E16.
  5. The team trusts him. His manager thanked him in #maps for holding the fort while out of office E12. A designer thanked him for picking up an issue unprompted E13. A teammate's end-of-week thanks in #maps on 23 Oct named him and one other colleague E19. After the 27 Oct incident, his manager credited him by name in an engineering-wide #eng write-up E21. On 6 Nov his manager gave him and the designer a shout-out in #applied-experiments for improving user experience, and the designer thanked him for paving the way for data ingestion E29. This week he approved two of his manager's PRs, both reviewed at the manager's request E1. He set up a Linear loop that posts each release's notes to #maps, and shared two agent skills he built in a division-wide #aed-eng thread E28. His ingestion demo drew a 63-reply thread E14, and 410 of his roughly 560 public Slack messages are in #maps.

  • The flagship ingestion work has not merged two months after he announced it, and the open v2 PR is too large to review in one pass.
  • His merged-PR rate fell below the team's 75th percentile this week (2.78 against 2.99, n = 7), with 2 merges in W45. Part of the rise in p75 comes from a contributor whose first PR was 3 Nov: 1 merge in 2 days counts as 3.5 a week.
  • 18 Linear issues are in a started state at once, and MAPS-606 was reopened after being counted done.
  • He has reviewed 5 teammates' PRs, two of them this week. That is above the team median of 0, but still small.
  • One merged PR was reverted the same day.
  • Ownership transfer has 2 production events since its 8 Oct launch. The sidebar-pick event from #911 never appears in production. The usage figures shown until 23 Oct were test traffic and have been withdrawn.
  • Stacked-marker adoption rests on eleven days of data, with no before-number. Its tracking code threw an error on an older browser.
  • Smarter search slipped from the week of 2 Nov to 11 or 12 Nov, and its code review is unfinished.
  • Who owns ingestion is not confirmed in writing, though Linear lists him again as of 5 Nov.
  • A Notion summary says #825 shipped behind a flag. GitHub shows it closed without merging.
Before review

  1. Land ingestion in slices, and settle who owns it. Split #907 into reviewable PRs behind the existing custom-csv-import flag, and confirm in writing that he owns the flow (Linear lists him on MAPS-471 as of 5 Nov) against the end-of-November target. A merged flagship outweighs everything else on this page.
  2. Get the after-number for ingestion. His UT plan set a baseline of 25m 11s at the upload step and a 41% error rate. Re-measure both once it ships.
  3. Find out why two of his events barely register in production. Check whether map_ownership_transferred fired in production before 29 Oct, and why search_suggestion_selected from #911 is missing there. Either a quiet feature or broken tracking is worth knowing before review. While there, replace toSorted in the #922 tracking so older browsers stop throwing.
  4. Keep the review habit going. He approved #957 and #964 this week, taking him from 3 reviews to 5. One or two a week until review would turn the thinnest signal here into a steady one.
  5. Close or hand back in-flight issues to bring the 18 started Linear issues down, and ask the manager and designer for written feedback to attach.
